Right concepts. Clear thinking. Better tools.

CodeRenewables is my personal initiative to give engineers the right concepts, ways of thinking and tools for resource assessment in wind and solar, so energy estimates become more accurate and better understood.

Explore the tools View on GitHub

What it’s about

In resource assessment, small assumptions decide large numbers. Everything here serves one of three aims.

Concepts

The ideas behind the methods, such as shear, long-term correction, transposition and uncertainty, explained plainly with the standards and sources behind them.

Thinking

How to reason about the numbers: which assumptions matter most, how errors combine, and when an energy estimate deserves trust.

Tools

Free browser tools that put the concepts to work, with methods you can read and check. Much of the code is open on GitHub.

Tools

Live web apps you can use right now in your browser.

WattOdds

wattodds.coderenewables.com

Get the odds on your energy yield. Explore the probabilities behind a project's expected output (P50, P90 and beyond) and see which uncertainties drive the risk.

Open WattOdds →

Sitebook

sitebook.coderenewables.com

One organised record for each renewable site. Bring together measurement data, metadata and documentation so the whole team works from the same source of truth.

Open Sitebook →

WindNudge

windnudge.coderenewables.com

Nudge one assumption and see how far your wind yield moves. It reruns every scenario through measured wind speed data and ranks what matters most: speed, shear, air density or losses.

Open WindNudge →

SolarNudge

solarnudge.coderenewables.com

Nudge one assumption and see how far your PV yield moves. It reruns every scenario through hourly irradiance data and ranks what matters most: irradiance, soiling, temperature, clipping and more.

Open SolarNudge →

LossStack

lossstack.coderenewables.com

See where your energy goes between gross and the meter. Build a wind or solar plant's loss stack with WattOdds' loss items, find which losses carry the uncertainty, and open the stack in WattOdds for P50 to P99.

Open LossStack →
